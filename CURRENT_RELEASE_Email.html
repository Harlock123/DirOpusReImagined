DirOpus Reimagined v0.1.36.0
A fast, cross-platform dual-panel file manager — inspired by the classic Amiga Directory Opus.

Hi! I've been building a file manager and just released a new version — thought you might like to try it. It runs on Windows, macOS, and Linux (Intel & ARM), it's free and open source, and there's nothing to install — just unzip and run.

What it does
  • Two folders side by side — copy, move, and compare between them, with folder tabs on each side.
  • All the file basics — copy / move / rename / delete, drag-and-drop, copy and paste with other file managers, delete-to-Trash (recoverable), and undo for renames, moves and deletes.
  • Compare & sync two folders with a click, compare two files side by side, and find duplicates.
  • Cloud storage built in — Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox, Amazon S3, and 50+ more, plus your own SFTP, SMB, FTP and WebDAV servers, browsed like local folders.
  • Archives like folders — open .zip, .7z, .rar, and .tar, copy files into, rename and delete inside .zip and .tar archives, and save edits back into them — even archives inside archives, password-protected zips and .iso disk images.
  • Batch rename — rename a whole selection with a live before→after preview, find&replace (regex), and numbering.
  • Make it yours — banks of 36 custom buttons to launch your own tools, plus bookmarks, a folder tree, a command line, and a built-in file viewer and hex editor.
  • Themes — Light/Dark, Dracula, Nord, and Solarized.
  • Fast to drive — search by name or by what’s inside files, recent folders, flat view, a command palette, type-ahead jumping, and full keyboard control with your own shortcuts.
New in 0.1.36:
  • Find and replace in files — change text across a whole folder (plain, whole words or regex), with every changed line shown first and undo after.
  • Trash browser — see what’s in the system trash, put things back where they came from, or empty it.
  • Slideshow and photo sorting — step through a folder in the viewer with the arrow keys, or show its pictures full screen: label with 1–7, Delete to trash, R to turn.
  • Hex editor — open any file, however large, go to an offset, find bytes or text, and change bytes in place with undo (Shift+F3).
  • Convert audio and video — make MP3, AAC, FLAC, MP4, WebM or GIF files, cut out a part, or shrink a video, with ffmpeg.
  • Connect to Server — open SFTP, Windows shares (SMB), FTP and WebDAV servers from just an address and login.
  • Drives and devices — free space in the drive menu, one-click eject for USB sticks and cards, and an offer to open a drive when it’s plugged in.
  • Labels other apps can see — on Linux and macOS, colour labels and tags are kept on the files too, so Dolphin and Finder show them (and DORI shows theirs).
  • Open folders in DORI from anywhere — “Open With → DORI” on Linux, “Open in DORI” in Explorer on Windows; folders open as new tabs in the DORI already running.
  • Check for updates — find out when a new version is out, by hand or once a day (off until you turn it on).
  • Earlier, in 0.1.28 to 0.1.35: a folder tree, a command line, renaming by photo date and music tags, password-protected zips, .iso disk images, quick editing in the viewer, saved sync jobs, Go to Folder, drag and drop with other apps, copy and paste with other file managers, folder rules, disk catalogues, archives inside archives, a speed limit and Start Later, folder sizes, natural sort, video thumbnails, exporting listings, secure delete, disk usage, labels and tags, a git status column, image tools, synced browsing, copies that carry on, splitting and joining files, undo for deletes, renames and moves, comparing files, flat view, finding duplicates, the command palette, saved layouts and filters, your own shortcuts, photo details, search inside files, banks of buttons and checksums.
Fixed: since 0.1.19, copying or moving to and from cloud folders, and copying or moving files out of archives, didn’t work. 0.1.28 fixed that, and 0.1.29 fixes a stray line left around the active panel after resizing the window, and button labels cut off in small windows.
